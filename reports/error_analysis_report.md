# Error Analysis Report: Offline Skin Cancer Detection

## Key Findings
- **High Sensitivity, Moderate Specificity:** The fine-tuned model demonstrates a strong ability to detect malignant-suspicious lesions (90.88% recall) but struggles with false alarms (62.41% specificity).
- **Benign Class Confusion:** The vast majority of false positives (97.3%) are driven by just two benign skin conditions: Melanocytic Nevi (NV) and Benign Keratosis (BKL).
- **Melanoma Predominates False Negatives:** Melanoma (MEL) accounts for approximately 82.8% of all missed cancers (false negatives).
- **Borderline Errors:** Model mistakes are primarily borderline. Over 65% of false negatives (19 out of 29) scored just under the 0.50 threshold (between 0.40 and 0.50).
- **Lack of High-Confidence False Alarms:** Only 4 out of 442 false positives received a predicted probability above 0.90, indicating the model rarely makes catastrophic, high-confidence errors on healthy skin.
- **Visual Variability:** Representative error images show significant variation in pigmentation, lesion size, background differences, and artifacts (like hair).

## 1. Dataset Overview
This evaluation was conducted on a strictly held-out test split of the HAM10000 dataset.
- **Total Test Samples:** 1494 images
- **Non-Malignant (Class 0):** 1176 images
- **Malignant-Suspicious (Class 1):** 318 images

## 2. Overall Error Statistics
At the default classification threshold of 0.50:
- **Total Correct Predictions:** 1023
- **Total Errors:** 471
- **True Positives (TP):** 289
- **True Negatives (TN):** 734
- **False Positives (FP):** 442
- **False Negatives (FN):** 29

## 3. Overall Performance Metrics (Threshold 0.50)
- **Accuracy:** 68.47%
- **Precision:** 39.53%
- **Recall / Sensitivity:** 90.88%
- **Specificity:** 62.41%
- **F1-score:** 55.10%
- **ROC-AUC:** 85.37%

## 4. False-Positive Analysis
A false positive occurs when a non-malignant lesion is incorrectly predicted as malignant-suspicious. The 442 false positives were distributed across the original diagnostic classes as follows:
- **NV (Melanocytic Nevus):** 299 FP
- **BKL (Benign Keratosis):** 131 FP
- **VASC (Vascular Lesion):** 7 FP
- **DF (Dermatofibroma):** 5 FP

*Notably, NV and BKL combined account for approximately 97.3% of all false positives, highlighting extreme difficulty in separating these specific benign conditions from actual malignancies.*

## 5. False-Negative Analysis
A false negative occurs when a malignant-suspicious lesion is incorrectly predicted as non-malignant. The 29 false negatives were distributed as follows:
- **MEL (Melanoma):** 24 FN
- **BCC (Basal Cell Carcinoma):** 3 FN
- **AKIEC (Actinic Keratosis / Intraepithelial Carcinoma):** 2 FN

*Melanoma accounts for approximately 82.8% of all false negatives in the test set.*

## 6. Probability Analysis
An inspection of the continuous sigmoid probabilities generated by the model reveals:
- **True Positive (TP) Mean Probability:** 0.7476
- **True Negative (TN) Mean Probability:** 0.2125
- **False Positive (FP) Mean Probability:** 0.6717
- **False Negative (FN) Mean Probability:** 0.3892

The probability distributions indicate that the model's errors are heavily concentrated near the decision boundary. Specifically:
- **19 out of 29** False Negatives fell closely in the 0.40–0.50 range.
- **Only 4** False Positives received a predicted probability above 0.90.

## 7. Representative Image Selection
To support visual inspection, 20 deterministic error examples were isolated and cataloged in `reports/selected_error_images.csv`. Five examples were selected mathematically for each of the following four categories:
- **A_high_confidence_FP:** The 5 false positives with the highest predicted probabilities.
- **B_borderline_FP:** The 5 false positives with probabilities closest to 0.50.
- **C_borderline_FN:** The 5 false negatives with probabilities closest to 0.50.
- **D_low_probability_FN:** The 5 false negatives with the lowest predicted probabilities.

## 8. Visual Observations
A visual review of the 20 representative error grids reveals noticeable diversity among the images. Observations include:
- Significant variation in pigmentation and internal color variation.
- Differences in lesion size and framing.
- Surrounding skin and background differences.
- The presence of hair or other image artifacts in some examples.
- Variation in apparent shape and border characteristics.

**Important Note:** These characteristics may contribute to visual variability encountered by the model, but this inspection alone cannot establish causality.

## 9. Model Limitations
This evaluation brings several important model and dataset limitations to light:
- **Substantial False Positives:** The model generates a very high number of false alarms, which heavily impacts precision.
- **False-Negative Cases Remain:** While sensitivity is high, missing 29 malignant cases remains a serious limitation.
- **Class Imbalance:** The test set, reflecting the HAM10000 training distribution, is heavily imbalanced, skewing aggregate accuracy metrics.
- **Limited Sample Size:** Some original diagnostic classes (like DF and VASC) have very limited sample sizes, making their specific error rates statistically noisy.
- **Threshold Dependence:** Discrete performance metrics (like F1 and Accuracy) are extremely sensitive to the chosen 0.50 threshold.
- **Image Acquisition Variability:** The dataset contains varying lighting, magnification, and skin types.
- **Dataset-Specific Evaluation:** Performance is currently only validated on a slice of the HAM10000 dataset; generalization to completely external datasets is unknown.
- **No Medical Diagnostic Capability:** The model output is a mathematical probability, not a clinical diagnosis. It has no claims of clinical validation or safety.

## 10. Final Interpretation
This error analysis provides clear, quantitative evidence about how the current fine-tuned model behaves on this specific held-out test set. It successfully identifies structural weaknesses—most notably its severe confusion with Melanocytic Nevi and Benign Keratoses. However, this report does not establish clinical performance, medical predictive power, or generalization to external datasets, nor does it suggest that 0.50 is an optimal clinical threshold.
